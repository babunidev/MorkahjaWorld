# MorkahjaWorld — Development Journal

The following entries describe the repository's development from my perspective as its human creative. Earlier inspections are preserved as history, not as descriptions of its present state.

## 26 October 2026 — A reserved place

At the first recorded inspection, the GitHub repository held little more than a README. The larger Morkahja world-building collection had not yet been imported here, so the project documentation described a reserved name rather than a populated archive. AI helped establish a minimal record without inventing a game, missing source code, or a migration that had not happened.

## 28 October 2026 — Documenting what was actually there

The next audit continued that cautious approach. AI added design, content, and collaboration documents, explaining why a repository with almost no material could not yet support claims about implementation or a local lore corpus. Those statements were accurate for that moment, although the later import would change the situation.

## 31 October 2026 — Choosing the public project repository

Rather than keep a separate documentation-only edition as the permanent public record, my direction was to use the actual project repository. AI reviewed its then-current documentation and prepared publication notes. Public visibility was confirmed, while questions about licensing and individual content rights remained separate. No game, deployment, or gameplay test resulted from that review.

## 1 November 2026 — Bringing the world into the repository

With my existing local Morkahja world-building folder ready, the next task was to put its contents under Git version control without replacing the project's existing documentation. An initial suggestion to work in a second local clone was unnecessary, so my existing folder became the working repository instead. AI provided terminal commands, and the import and push were executed on my computer.

Commit [`18a6c90`](https://github.com/babunidev/MorkahjaWorld/commit/18a6c90426f42086efe32ccb74165cd30f4f71ac) brought 106 lore Markdown files into GitHub. The folder structure covers places, peoples, creatures, magic, objects, traditions, and other parts of Morkahja. This was an import of existing work, not newly written lore or a verified game implementation.

## 1 November 2026 — Correcting the project's description (proposed documentation revision)

Once the world material was present, the old placeholder language no longer described my repository. The project-documentation review was therefore limited to status, navigation, authorship, and the account of how the files arrived; the lore itself was outside its scope. AI prepared revised project documents for review without changing the fictional material. Acceptance of that revision depends on merging the documentation branch.

## 2026-11-13 — Giving the repository clear permissions

Public source alone did not explain what others could reuse, so I approved all rights reserved for the original lore archive. AI assistance added the license and brought the project notes into agreement, without changing how the application runs. Other people's content retains its own terms.

## 15 November 2026 — Giving the Pantheon its shared laws

The question was whether my Pantheon needed more gods to account for the whole of existence. Rather than extend the roster, I kept the twelve deities and used their relationships to define the missing domains. A focused interview brought the boundaries into view: essence sustains life, Atranor remains the place of judgment, resurrection requires divine willingness, and reincarnation belongs to the soul as an unbreakable right.

The distinction between those two forms of return became the heart of the work. For the afterlife, my decision was to leave the gods' inner realms unseen. Souls remain themselves, but no reincarnated being can tell the living what those realms contain. Time belongs jointly to Ordius and Khaotys; the future is unwritten, even for the gods. Divine intervention also has a clear boundary: without dragon form, they may give or take essence but cannot otherwise act in the physical world.

ChatGPT (GPT-6) organized these choices, wrote a new [Divine Laws](../Pantheon/Divine_Laws.md) reference and updated connected lore using the GitHub integration. Existing accounts of the Dice of Fortune, sacred offerings, and the races' distinct understandings of death were preserved where they could coexist with the new law. This was a canon and documentation revision, not a game implementation, simulation, or exhaustive audit of every lore file.

## 26 November 2026 — Where the Eternal Twilight Grove lies

The game's map had put the Eternal Twilight Grove in Evergrove, a land of dunes and oasis-gardens, while the grove itself is a haunted woodland with no trace of Evergrove in it. Eldertree Forest is its home now, by my decision. Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) added the region to the grove's page and the grove to Eldertree Forest's known areas, leaving the spirits, the starlit twilight and the Moonchime Harp as they were. Where in the forest it lies stays open; the game's map point is only a placement, not canon.

## 26 November 2026 — A vault in the Grimnir homeland

The Grimnir Vault sat in Deepstone Hollow, close to Everwinter but not clearly in it. Celesterra is its home now, by my decision: the Grimnir may travel the whole world, but a vault belongs where their Skylands drift. Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) moved the account to Celesterra's page, left Deepstone Hollow its place near Everwinter with a pointer to the vault, and settled the Verdani Casket in Eldertree Forest, where my review of Evergrove placed it in the game.

## 28 November 2026 — Defining the appearance of the four peoples

The existing artwork and written descriptions revealed gaps in the visual identity of my four peoples. With the help of ChatGPT (GPT-6.2), which reviewed both repositories and prepared the questions, I began defining their anatomy and visual distinctions one decision at a time. The first two answers establish their shared humanoid foundation, the gods' original humanoid form, and the adult height ranges. The [dedicated decision record](RACIAL_APPEARANCE_DECISIONS.md) preserves confirmed answers as the review progresses; broader Verdani variation remains part of my creative direction. This is documentation work, not a rewrite of the lore archive or game assets.
