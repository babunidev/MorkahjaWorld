# Blackwood Abyss

[Places index](Places_Index.md) · [All lore](../../README.md)

**Location:** Beneath the cursed forest of [Keldor](Keldor.md)  
**Classification:** Forbidden region, living labyrinth, ancient entity site  
**Known access point:** The Blackwood Portal  
**Related world event:** [The Urcogor Ravage](../World_Events/Urcogor_Ravage.md)  
**Elemental reading:** Darkness, Void, Nature, Spirit, corrupted memory

The Blackwood Abyss is one of [Keldor](Keldor.md)'s oldest and least understood anomalies. Hidden beneath the cursed wilderness of the forest, it exists as both place and presence, defying ordinary geography, magic, and life.

To some, it is a forgotten ruin swallowed by corruption. To others, it is an ancient wound beneath the world. Survivors rarely agree on what they witnessed, but many return with the same impossible details: roots that breathe, roads that remember, faces in bark, water that reflects wrong, and a throne that waits in silence.

## Overview

The abyss is reached through the Blackwood Portal, an elusive gateway concealed within remote cliffs deep inside Keldor. Finding the portal is extraordinarily rare. Many search for it and fail. Those who discover it often cannot locate it again.

Stranger still, most who reach the door never open it.

The Blackwood Abyss has shaped myths, disappearances, and centuries of fear along Keldor's borders. Local hunters, [Verdani](../Races/Verdani.md) healers, and scattered scholars all record fragments of the same warning: whatever lies beneath the forest is not simply underground.

It is aware.

## The Cursed Forest Above

The forest surrounding the portal acts as both barrier and warning.

Keldor is a hostile wilderness enclosed by distant mountain ranges and blanketed in permanent fog. The curse affecting the region alters vegetation, wildlife, and terrain, twisting natural forms into warped reflections of themselves. Trees grow in unnatural directions. Animal behavior becomes erratic. Travelers report memory loss, distorted time, and a persistent sense of being watched.

The deeper regions closest to the portal are especially unstable. There, physical corruption gives way to psychological intrusion. Visitors describe distant voices, impossible structures glimpsed between trees, and memories that no longer feel entirely their own.

Many disappear within Keldor.

Those who return often refuse to speak of what they saw.

## The Blackwood Portal

The Blackwood Portal is the only known entrance to the abyss.

It appears as a solitary wooden door embedded high within a dark cliff face. Ancient runes cover its surface, though witnesses claim the markings shift subtly whenever directly observed. The door radiates a persistent sense of wrongness that many struggle to explain.

Most individuals cannot open it.

No confirmed mechanism has been documented. Theories suggest passwords, relics, bloodlines, magical resonance, specific emotional states, or the portal choosing who may pass. More disturbing are reports from those who never entered at all.

Exposure alone appears dangerous.

Travelers who stand near the portal frequently describe whispers, emotional distress, intrusive thoughts, nausea, memory disruption, and an overwhelming instinct to flee. Many report that merely standing nearby creates the sensation that something beyond the door has become aware of them.

The common saying among Keldor's hunters remains:

"Do not touch the door. Do not listen to the door. And if it notices you, run."

## Nature of the Abyss

Early theories described the Blackwood Abyss as a dungeon system beneath Keldor.

Modern interpretations reject this.

Accounts suggest the abyss behaves less like architecture and more like anatomy. Roots function as corridors. Caverns resemble chambers within a larger organism. Pulses travel through walls. Terrain shifts without explanation. Distances stretch or collapse unpredictably.

Many scholars classify the abyss as a dormant biological structure of incomprehensible scale. The dominant theory proposes that an immense ancient lifeform lies buried beneath Keldor, and that the Blackwood Abyss is not built inside it.

It is the creature.

## Elemental Interpretation

The Blackwood Abyss does not align cleanly to a single tribunal. Its pattern suggests several forces in unstable relationship:

- **Darkness** hides its true nature, guards buried secrets, and distorts perception beneath sight.
- **Void** bends distance, separation, silence, and the reliability of maps.
- **Nature** appears in the living root-architecture, but twisted away from healthy growth.
- **Spirit** appears through voices, memory echoes, dreamlike intrusions, and the possibility of trapped consciousness.
- **Corruption** binds these forces into a predatory or defensive structure.

Because of this, scholars disagree on whether the abyss is a corrupted natural organism, an ancient prison, a spiritual wound, or a living threshold to something deeper than the known world.

The abyss is sometimes named in theories about the Urcogor Ravage. Those theories claim that places where reality feels thin can gather an ancient presence capable of waking the sleeping blood of the Urco, Gobbls, Ogrus, and their hybrid kin.

## Major Regions

### Tangled Depths

The Tangled Depths form the outer layers of the abyss.

Colossal roots dominate the environment, creating tunnels, bridges, arches, and endless mazes of intertwined wood. Visibility remains poor despite the absence of complete darkness, as the environment absorbs sound and distorts spatial awareness.

Navigation becomes nearly impossible. Travelers report returning to locations they never revisited or discovering paths changing behind them.

The roots themselves occasionally shift.

Whether this movement is natural, magical, or intentional remains unknown.

### The Hollow Choir

The Hollow Choir consists of suspended forests where enormous trees hang upside down from unseen ceilings.

Faces appear embedded within bark surfaces.

Witnesses consistently describe hearing music. The source remains disputed. Some claim the songs originate from trapped souls. Others believe the abyss itself communicates through resonance and memory.

Prolonged exposure often results in auditory hallucinations, grief responses, and emotional instability.

### The Weeping Mire

The Weeping Mire is a subterranean marsh of black water and submerged stone figures.

Pools within the mire produce abnormal reflections that diverge from reality. Observers sometimes report alternate expressions, injuries, or movements appearing independently within mirrored surfaces.

The swamp contains numerous partially buried statues. No evidence confirms whether they were carved or transformed.

Sounds resembling quiet crying frequently emerge beneath the water.

Excavation attempts have never succeeded.

### The Blackwood Guardian

Deeper passage into the abyss is blocked by the [Blackwood Guardian](../Creatures/Guardians_and_Spirits.md#blackwood-guardian).

Descriptions vary, though most identify it as an immense black tree with mobile roots and limited awareness. The Guardian appears less like a creature and more like a biological checkpoint embedded into the abyss itself.

Entry beyond this point requires unknown conditions. Some accounts mention passwords. Others mention objects, offerings, blood memory, or recognition by the abyss.

None agree.

### The Forgotten Procession

A preserved stone road cuts through deeper regions where endless hooded figures march in silence.

No beginning or end to the procession has been recorded. Travelers frequently identify individuals among the figures that resemble themselves.

The entities ignore interaction attempts. Their destination is always the same.

The Silent Throne.

### The Silent Throne

At the center of the abyss stands a cathedral-like chamber grown from roots, petrified wood, and unknown organic structures.

Thousands of stone figures kneel toward a throne occupied by an ancient robed being. Behind the throne rises an enormous root structure that pulses rhythmically.

Most survivors describe a realization rather than a discovery here:

The abyss is alive.

The seated figure is believed not to rule this place. Its purpose appears to be containment, vigilance, or unwilling union with the entity beneath Keldor.

Records consistently mention one final detail.

The figure eventually opens its eyes.

## Verdani Accounts

A small number of survivors have reportedly been recovered near Keldor's forest border, often unconscious, severely injured, or psychologically altered.

Several cases involve treatment by Verdani healers stationed near Keldor's outer regions. These healers describe survivor trauma as neither ordinary injury nor simple magical corruption. Dreams blend with memory. Reality becomes fragmented. Some survivors later insist the abyss never existed. Others become obsessed with returning.

Many continue reporting sensations of deep vibrations beneath the earth years afterward.

Verdani healers are divided on the nature of these cases. Some believe the abyss infects the Currentheart-like memory patterns of living beings. Others argue that survivors briefly become part of the abyss's own memory and never fully separate again.

## Scholarly Interpretations

Three dominant theories exist.

**The Buried Entity Theory** claims that Keldor sits atop an ancient organism older than recorded civilization.

**The Dream Theory** argues that the abyss exists primarily as a psychic or spiritual space accessed through consciousness.

**The Containment Theory** proposes that the throne, Guardian, and portal exist to prevent something beneath Keldor from waking.

No theory fully explains the portal. None explain why survivors share identical details. None explain why maps of Keldor become inaccurate near the portal's last known locations.

The Blackwood Abyss remains officially unclassified by most institutions.

Many prefer it that way.

## Open Possibilities

- The Blackwood Portal may choose entrants through resonance, ancestry, memory, or corruption.
- The abyss may be a living organism, a prison, a dream-space, or all three.
- The Silent Throne's robed figure may be a guardian, prisoner, former ruler, or consciousness-anchor.
- Verdani healers near Keldor may hold the most reliable survivor records.
- [Liani Shadowwolf](../Creatures/Wolves_and_Liani.md#liani-shadowwolf) may know whether the abyss is a truth hidden by Umbrael's darkness or a corruption that should never be remembered.
- Maps of Keldor fail near the portal because space itself may be folded by Void influence.
- The Blackwood Guardian could be a gatekeeper or misunderstood immune response of the buried entity.
- The abyss may be a source, amplifier, or prison for the ancient presence behind the Urcogor Ravage.

## Root Paths

The Tangled Depths contain arches and tunnels formed by enormous roots, with sudden drops beneath their interwoven paths. Creaking wood and unseen movement make it difficult to distinguish a safe passage from a living obstruction.

## Faces of the Hollow Choir

Faces embedded in the inverted trees move their mouths with the sorrowful singing. Fragments of forgotten names and memories travel in the voices. The faces appear to watch passing travelers, while the surrounding darkness swallows ordinary footsteps.

## Reflections of the Weeping Mire

The black pools reflect a sky absent from the cavern above. Reflected faces may lack eyes or display expressions their owners do not share. Sobbing seems to rise from directly beneath a listener's feet; the nature of the submerged stone figures remains uncertain.

## Lanterns of the Forgotten Procession

The hooded marchers carry extinguished lanterns and continue without acknowledging interference. Travelers report seeing their own build, dress, and movements among the procession, sometimes in a figure walking near its front.

## The Keeper and the Great Root

The Silent Throne is veined with dim crimson light. Behind its dust-covered keeper, a vast root pulses like an artery, sending tremors through the floor. Some accounts identify the cathedral as an organ within a buried being and the seated figure as the keeper of its sleep. This remains an interpretation of the abyss, not an established connection to [Ethera](Ethera.md).

## The Cliff Door

The Blackwood Portal is a wooden door set high in a dark Keldor cliff. Its runes appear to move in changing light, and whispers can be heard beyond it. Those approaching describe an oppressive sense of awareness rather than an ordinary sealed doorway.
