# Eldertree Forest

[Places index](Places_Index.md) · [All lore](../../README.md)

**Type:** Ancient forest region  
**Location:** Southern central Morkahja  
**Known inhabitants:** Liani, forest creatures, hidden spirits  
**Elemental reading:** Nature, Water, Spirit, Growth, living memory

Eldertree Forest is an ancient woodland of enormous trees, secluded clearings, streams, and deep natural magic. Its oldest growth forms a canopy that changes the light beneath it, creating warm dells, shadowed hollows, and green chambers where the boundary between ordinary wilderness and living spirit becomes difficult to identify.

The forest is associated with the Liani and with traditions of guardianship, animal care, seasonal rites, and patient coexistence. Travelers who enter respectfully may find peaceful groves and clear water. Those who treat the forest as an unclaimed resource often become lost among paths that appear to rearrange themselves.

## Known Areas

- **[Eldertree Grove](Eldertree_Landmarks.md#eldertree-grove):** A quiet grove beneath ancient trees, used for rest and seasonal gatherings.
- **[Shadowed Hollow](Eldertree_Landmarks.md#shadowed-hollow):** A secluded region where little sunlight reaches the ground and old memories seem to cling to the roots.
- **[Sunlit Dell](Eldertree_Landmarks.md#sunlit-dell):** A warm clearing where filtered light supports rare flowers and gentle creatures.
- **[Firefly Brook](Eldertree_Landmarks.md#firefly-brook):** A narrow stream illuminated at night by countless glowing insects.
- **[Grimnir Steps](Eldertree_Landmarks.md#grimnir-steps):** Ancient stone formations whose origin and connection to the [Grimnir](../Races/Grimnir.md) remain uncertain.

## Open Possibilities

- The forest may preserve memories older than the current Pantheon-created civilizations.
- The Liani may guard paths to places that cannot be reached by ordinary travel.
- The Grimnir Steps may be remnants of an early alliance, a trade route, or an older civilization.
- A corrupted region such as Rotwood may represent a wound spreading toward the forest's deeper roots.
