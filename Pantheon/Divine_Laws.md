# Divine Laws of Morkahja

[All lore](../Lore_Index.md) · [Elemental Pantheon](Elemental_Pantheon.md) · [Atranor](../Places/Atranor.md)

The twelve gods are not distant rulers standing apart from Morkahja. Their divine essences are woven through the world and sustain its living beings. The laws below describe their shared responsibilities and the boundaries of their power. They apply across the four tribunals; no new deity is required to govern these processes.

## Divine Essence and Mortal Life

Every living being is sustained by essence originating from the gods. Throughout life, essence may be received, given away, or taken by other creatures. Even a single remaining fragment can sustain life. Death comes when the last life-sustaining fragment is lost.

That final fragment returns to the deity to whom it belongs. The soul—the enduring, conscious self shaped by divine essence—passes into [Atranor](../Places/Atranor.md). The return of the final fragment and the soul's passage are related, but they are not the same event: the soul does not cease to exist.

The many elemental ways in which bodies fail, dissolve, or extinguish differ among Morkahja's peoples. These describe the end of a particular form, not an exception to the passage of the soul.

## Judgment and Resurrection

In Atranor, three deities hold a particular role in a soul's judgment: **Aerisara**, Goddess of Spirit; **Sylvanus**, God of Growth; and **the deity whose essence the being held last**. If the last fragment belonged to Ferrumis, Ferrumis is the third judge.

The soul's mortal life is revealed and considered. A judgment may offer **resurrection**, a return to the same life with identity and memories intact. Resurrection requires a gift of life-sustaining essence from **at least one** god. One deity may give it, several may give it, all twelve may give it, or none may choose to do so. It does not require a unanimous gift. Without any such gift, resurrection does not occur.

The four [Atranor Items](../Places/Atranor.md#the-atranor-items) may be offered during judgment, and the [Dice of Fortune](../Places/Atranor.md#the-dice-of-fortune) remain part of Atranor's mystery. Neither is a guaranteed formula for a divine gift.

## The Right to Reincarnation

**Every soul has an unconditional right to reincarnate.** The Pantheon unanimously upholds this rule. No deity may hold a soul within its realm against its wish to begin another life.

A soul that is not resurrected enters the inner realm of the deity to whom its final essence returned. It unites elementally with that god without losing its distinct consciousness, identity, memory, or free will. A soul may remain there for as long as it chooses or choose to reincarnate. Each deity's inner realm is different, but its experience and conditions remain deliberately unknowable to the living.

When a soul chooses reincarnation, it chooses its new mortal identity, including its people, appearance, and form. It is not bound to the people to whom it belonged in a former life. **Several gods share the gift of essence** that sustains this new incarnation; none may deny the soul's right to begin again. A reincarnated soul does not remember its former lives or the divine realms. Traces of earlier mortal lives may nevertheless appear as affinities, instincts, dreams, emotions, or recognition without explanation.

Resurrection and reincarnation must not be confused: resurrection restores a previous life at divine discretion; reincarnation begins a new life by the soul's own choice. The physical manner in which a chosen new form first enters the world is not established.

## Time and the Unwritten Future

**Ordius** and **Khaotys** are Morkahja's principal keepers of time. Ordius sustains continuity, sequence, and order; Khaotys sustains change, uncertainty, and the possibilities that arise within time. Their forces complement one another. Other gods participate in the cycles of existence, but do not replace these two as timekeepers.

**Destiny does not exist.** No being follows a predetermined future. Choices, circumstances, and unpredictable events continually shape what comes next. The future is not yet a thing that can be witnessed: **not even the gods can see it**. No prophecy can provide certain knowledge of an event that must occur. The ability to manipulate time itself has not been defined.

## The Nature and Conduct of the Gods

The gods cannot be destroyed, because they are inseparable from the world and its fundamental forces. They are not immutable: their immense accumulation of essence means that significant changes in their character or expression occur only across extraordinarily long spans.

The twelve gods cooperate closely. They may hold different views, but resolve disagreements without pursuing rival agendas or deliberately working against one another. Order and Chaos are complementary principles, not enemies.

In their unmanifested divine state the gods can **give or take essence**, but cannot otherwise act directly within the physical world. To enter and interact with Morkahja, a god must assume its **dragon form**. Such visits are familiar rather than legendary impossibilities—occasional, roughly as common as holidays, without any established fixed schedule.

## Shared Domains of Mortal Experience

The twelve gods also express broader aspects of mortal existence through cooperation. These are **shared responsibilities**, not additional gods or new tribunals.

| Aspect | Divine relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Love and relationships | **Aerisara** nurtures spiritual and emotional bonds; **Fyraan** embodies warmth, passion, and desire. |
| Dreams and visions | **Umbrael** holds the hidden mind and obscured memory; **Aetheria** inspires imagination and extraordinary visions. Dreams do not reveal a fixed future. |
| Decay and endings | **Sylvanus** governs natural decomposition and renewal; **Khaotys** governs dissolution and unpredictable transformation. |
| War and conflict | **Fyraan** embodies courage and fighting spirit; **Ferrumis** governs weapons, armor, and martial craft. Neither requires mortals to wage war. |
| Peace and reconciliation | **Ordius** governs just agreements and stable peace; **Nereval** governs healing, understanding, and forgiveness. |
| Memory and ancestry | **Umbrael** holds hidden histories and ancestral secrets; **Aerisara** sustains personal memory and bonds across generations. |
| Art, music, and creativity | **Fyraan** embodies creative passion and expression; **Aetheria** inspires imagination and possibility. |
| Luck and misfortune | **Khaotys** represents randomness and unexpected events; **Ordius** represents the patterns and circumstances through which outcomes arise. Neither predetermines them. |
| Travel and exploration | **Aeriel** embodies movement, freedom, and curiosity; **Nereval** sustains navigation and adaptation along the journey. |

## What Remains Unseen

The true conditions within each deity's inner realm are beyond the living world's knowledge. Whether souls meet there, what they experience, and what their elementally united existence feels like are intentionally left unwritten. Their memories of those realms do not return with reincarnation.

These mysteries do not weaken the laws of reincarnation or divine judgment. They mark the boundary between what Morkahja establishes and what no mortal can know.
