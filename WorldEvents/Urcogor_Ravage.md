# The Urcogor Ravage

**Type:** Recurring world event  
**Primary peoples:** Urco, Gobbls, Ogrus, Gobblogru, Ogrobbles  
**Known pattern:** Awakening, gathering, ravage, disappearance, dormancy  
**Elemental reading:** Force, Earth, Nature, Void, corrupted memory, ancient presence  
**Related places:** [Keldor](../Places/Keldor.md), [Blackwood Abyss](../Places/Blackwood_Abyss.md), ancient ruins, buried caverns, forgotten mountain halls

The Urcogor Ravage is a recurring period of upheaval in which ancient peoples emerge from hidden places, gather in growing numbers, and sweep across settled regions before disappearing again. It is not understood as a conventional war or invasion. No kingdom commands it, no known leader begins it, and no lasting state follows in its wake.

The Ravage begins with scattered signs: dormant clans awaken, lone Urco appear on forgotten roads, Gobbls return to abandoned ruins, and Ogrus are seen crossing remote mountain passes. These groups gradually move toward one another, often across impossible distances. As their numbers increase, raids become migrations, migrations become warbands, and the Ravage takes form.

When the event ends, the survivors withdraw into wilderness, ruins, underground networks, or territories beyond the known frontiers. Some enter a dormant state that may last for decades, centuries, or longer. Eventually, the cycle begins again.

## Place in Morkahja's History

The [Elemental Pantheon](../Pantheon/Elemental_Pantheon.md) is the accepted origin of the [Rokur](../Races/Rokur.md), [Verdani](../Races/Verdani.md), [Aevis](../Races/Aevis.md), and [Grimnir](../Races/Grimnir.md). Their societies reflect the four divine tribunals and the elemental order established by them.

The peoples of the Ravage do not fit within that creation tradition. Ancient ruins, tools, and buried settlements associated with the Urco, Gobbls, and Ogrus suggest that their bloodlines may descend from a civilization or world-order older than the present age. Their true origin is unknown, and no surviving record describes a first Ravage.

Some scholars believe the ancient peoples are remnants of a forgotten creation. Others suspect that they were shaped by forces that existed before the Pantheon, or that they are connected to an older form of elemental life. Most such theories remain speculative, but the age of the ruins associated with the Ravage is difficult to reconcile with ordinary history.

The Urco races are therefore not treated as a fifth Pantheon-created race. They are an older and less understood presence within Morkahja's history.

## The Cycle of the Ravage

The beginning of a Ravage has never been observed directly. The earliest reports already describe awakened groups in motion, suggesting that the process begins in remote places beyond the reach of settled peoples.

The cycle is generally understood to have five stages:

1. **Awakening:** Dormant clans emerge from buried chambers, ruins, mountains, forests, or underground networks.
2. **Gathering:** Separate groups begin moving toward one another, apparently responding to a shared instinct or distant call.
3. **Ravage:** The gathered peoples raid settlements, disrupt trade routes, occupy ruins, and drive populations from affected regions.
4. **Disappearance:** The gathered forces withdraw without establishing a permanent kingdom or unified territory.
5. **Dormancy:** Surviving clans return to hidden places and become inactive until the next recurrence.

The interval between Ravages is inconsistent. There is no confirmed calendar, celestial sign, or prophecy that reliably predicts the next awakening.

## The Peoples of the Ravage

The three primary peoples are the Urco, Gobbls, and Ogrus. Their mixed descendants include the Gobblogru and Ogrobbles. They share ancient origins and participate in the Ravage, but they are not a single unified culture.

### Urco

The Urco are generally regarded as the organizing force of the Ravage. They are physically powerful, durable, and deliberate in their movements, with dense builds, heavy features, and a strong tradition of clan loyalty. Their skin ranges from bronze and muted green to stone-grey, and many bear scars, ritual brands, or markings associated with forgotten lineages.

Urco leaders may serve as war chiefs, strategists, shamans, or keepers of ancestral memory. They are not necessarily the oldest or strongest members of a gathering, but they often provide its sense of direction. Their traditions appear to preserve fragments of history that have no equivalent in the records of younger civilizations.

### Gobbls

Gobbls are small, wiry, and highly adaptable. Their narrow frames, flexible movements, and dexterous hands allow them to move through ruins, cluttered settlements, and narrow underground passages with remarkable ease. Their skin varies between grey, green, brown, and mottled fungal tones, while their large reflective eyes are a common feature across many clans.

Gobbls are associated with scavenging, improvisation, and the recovery of objects from forgotten places. They are often the first members of a gathering to enter abandoned structures and the last to leave them. Their speed and unpredictability make them dangerous even when acting in small groups.

### Ogrus

Ogrus are massive beings whose bodies combine immense strength with unusual endurance. Their skin commonly resembles weathered earth or stone, and many develop hardened growths along the shoulders, back, and forearms. Their size and physical resilience make them the most visible force in an advancing Ravage.

Despite their reputation as destroyers, Ogrus are not mindless. They are capable of long memory, strong loyalty, and deliberate patience. Their speech and movements are often slow, but their role in a gathering is decisive: they provide the weight and persistence that allow a Ravage to continue after resistance has begun.

## Blurred Bloodlines

The ancient peoples have mingled across many generations, producing bloodlines that do not fit neatly into a single ancestry. Two of the most common are the Gobblogru and Ogrobbles.

### Gobblogru

Gobblogru combine the adaptability of Gobbls with the strength and resilience of Ogrus. They are usually compact but heavily built, with large reflective eyes, broad shoulders, dense limbs, and small tusks or uneven fangs. Their movements can shift quickly between stealth and sudden force.

They are among the most capable survivors of the Ravage, able to operate in confined spaces while carrying more strength than their size suggests.

### Ogrobbles

Ogrobbles are less consistent in appearance and behavior. Some resemble oversized Gobbls, while others possess shortened Ogru bodies with elongated limbs or unusual proportions. Asymmetry, extra joints, and unstable physical traits are common.

Their forms are often considered evidence that the ancient bloodlines do not follow the same developmental rules as the current Pantheon-created races. Ogrobbles are therefore both a recognized people and a living reminder of the Ravage's unknown origin.

## The Sleeping Blood

Dormancy is one of the defining traits of the ancient peoples. Entire clans have been found beneath mountains, in buried fortresses, beneath tangled forests, and within sealed caverns. Some appear to be preserved without aging. Others resemble statues or corpses until disturbed.

No reliable explanation exists for the sleep. It may be caused by ancient biology, elemental suspension, a deliberate survival practice, or an external force. The conditions that end it are equally uncertain. Relics, changes in the surrounding land, the presence of other awakened clans, and disturbances in hidden ruins have all been proposed as possible triggers.

Explorers are warned against disturbing dormant sites. Awakening one group may begin a chain of movement that eventually draws other clans toward it.

## The Ancient Call

Once awakened, the scattered peoples of the Ravage appear able to find one another without ordinary communication. They cross great distances, follow routes that are no longer visible, and converge on places that have no apparent strategic value.

Some scholars describe this as an inherited instinct. Others believe that the ancient peoples respond to a signal carried through stone, memory, or corrupted magic. A smaller and more troubling school of thought claims that Morkahja itself retains a memory of the older world and periodically calls its remnants back into motion.

The [Blackwood Abyss](../Places/Blackwood_Abyss.md) and the cursed forest of [Keldor](../Places/Keldor.md) are often named in these theories. Both locations contain signs of distorted memory, hidden spaces, and forces that seem older than the current age.

## Elemental Interpretation

The Urcogor Ravage does not belong to one of the four divine tribunals, but its effects can still be interpreted through the [Elemental Foundation](../Magic/Elemental_Foundation.md).

- **Force** appears in impact, mass movement, violence, and the momentum of gathering.
- **Earth** appears in buried chambers, dormant bodies, endurance, and the persistence of ancient memory.
- **Nature** appears in survival, adaptation, mutation, and the continuation of bloodlines.
- **Void** appears in disappearance, hidden territories, lost routes, and the separation of the sleepers from ordinary history.
- **Corrupted memory** appears in the return of patterns that no living culture can fully remember.

The Ravage may intersect with [Magic Corruption](../Magic/Magic_Corruption.md), but it should not be treated as a simple corrupted race event. Corruption distorts the current elemental order. The Ravage may instead be an older force pressing through that order, using damaged places as points of return.

## Known Connections

[Keldor](../Places/Keldor.md) is considered one of the most likely regions to contain dormant clans or traces of the ancient call. The [Blackwood Abyss](../Places/Blackwood_Abyss.md) may be a source, amplifier, or prison for the presence associated with the Ravage. The [Raided Verdani Village](../Places/Raided_Verdani_Village.md) is sometimes interpreted as a small local echo of the same pattern, though it does not prove that a full Ravage has begun.

These connections remain unconfirmed. The peoples of the Ravage may be drawn to such locations, or the locations may simply preserve evidence of their earlier movements.

## Open Possibilities

- A newly awakened clan may be the first sign of a future Ravage.
- The ancient peoples may be remnants of a world-order that predates the Pantheon.
- The Blackwood Abyss may contain the source of the call that awakens the sleeping blood.
- Keldor may conceal dormant Urco, Gobbls, Ogrus, or hybrid bloodlines beneath its roots.
- A relic from an ancient ruin may trigger a gathering without its bearer understanding why.
- Preventing a Ravage may require finding the source of the awakening rather than defeating the gathered peoples.
- The Ravage may not be evil in origin, but its recurrence is catastrophic for settled civilizations.
